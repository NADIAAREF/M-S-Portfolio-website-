Philosophy of Modeling & Simulation

Understanding complex systems through purposeful simplification.

I believe modeling and simulation is more than recreating real-world systems in a virtual environment. It is a scientific approach to understanding complexity, exploring possibilities, and making informed decisions. Every model represents a deliberate simplification of reality, and its value lies not in how much detail it captures, but in how effectively it addresses the question it was designed to answer.

Breadth — Choosing the right approach.

My experience with discrete-event simulation, system dynamics, and computational modeling has taught me that no single modeling approach is suitable for every problem. Whether examining customer flow in a banking system, studying technology adoption, or simulating infrared sensors for autonomous vehicles, the choice of methodology must be guided by the research objectives, system behavior, and available evidence. Understanding the strengths and limitations of different approaches is fundamental to meaningful simulation research.

Depth — Building confidence in models.

I view verification, validation, and uncertainty assessment as essential elements of responsible modeling and simulation. A technically sophisticated model is not necessarily a credible one. Its assumptions must be transparent, its implementation must be examined, and its outputs must be interpreted within the limitations of the available evidence. My research interests in artificial intelligence, scientific computing, and predictive modeling have reinforced the importance of combining computational innovation with scientific rigor and interpretability.

Purpose — Connecting simulation to human needs.

Ultimately, I believe the purpose of modeling and simulation is to generate knowledge that can improve real-world systems and human experiences. Whether supporting safer autonomous technologies, investigating anomalies in complex engineering systems, or developing intelligent learning environments, simulation should contribute to decisions that are informed, responsible, and meaningful. My work in human-centered modeling has particularly strengthened my appreciation for the cognitive, behavioral, and emotional dimensions that traditional technical models may overlook.

My perspective as a researcher.

As I continue my academic journey, I aspire to develop models that are not only computationally capable but also scientifically credible, interpretable, and relevant to real-world challenges. For me, successful modeling and simulation is not simply about predicting what may happen. It is about understanding why systems behave as they do, recognizing the limitations of our representations, and using that understanding to make better decisions.
